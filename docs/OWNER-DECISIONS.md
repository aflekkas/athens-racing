# Owner-locked decisions

These choices were made deliberately by the owner (Alexandros Lekkas). Don't change, revert or "improve" any of them without asking the owner first. Guard tests enforce several of them, and their failure messages point here. Add new entries at the bottom, dated, in the owner's words where possible.

2026-10-16 owner correction: **Cockpits must not be ghost cars.** This supersedes the floating thin-dash / blanket own-exterior hiding interpretation above. Every cockpit must show its own connected low-poly dash, inset instruments, bonnet/front deck, windscreen pillars and header/edge, door tops/window frames, and mounted mirrors. Convertibles retain their windscreen frame without a roof; bikes show tank/front fairing, bars and clocks. The lightweight inner shell is visible only in POV. Invisible glass and no occupants/hands remain locked. **The 80% pixel threshold was subsequently superseded by the owner clarification below.** `tools/cockpit-structure.test.mjs` independently recounts rendered part-ID pixels for all 70 configurations and rejects missing/hidden/off-screen structure (bonnet visibility follows real geometry; the CLA, Huracán, Jazz and cab-over truck can hide their front deck behind the cowl); `tools/pov-owner-lock.test.mjs` retains the clear-view and source-hash guard.

2026-10-16 owner addition: **“We need to be able to see the steering wheel.”** Every car's default POV has a visible blocky rim, hub and manufacturer badge from the shared atlas; the wheel rotates with actual steering input. Bus has a larger, flatter wheel; bikes have rotating handlebars. No hands or arms. The cockpit structure guard checks rendered rim/hub/badge pixels and steering movement across all 70 configurations.

2026-10-16 owner rain correction: **“I hope raindrops and puddles are really a texture overlay rather than real particles.”** Windscreen rain is one mesh sampling one shared 128px tiled alpha droplet texture, with small sparse drops and a wipe-mask uniform. No per-drop meshes, sprites, particles or rain-lens distortion. Wiper pivots attach to the cowl and blades sweep on the framed windscreen plane; the same sweep drives the alpha mask. World rain/puddles remain owned by the separate world-weather pass. Guarded by `tools/cockpit-structure.test.mjs`; same-pose rain before/after and draw-call evidence are in `.scratch/cockpit-structure/`.

2026-10-16 owner clarification and restoration: **“Each car keeps its own distinct interior; camera seated slightly high.”** “80% was more about where we sit in the car” and “they’re supposed to feel like I’m actually in the car, just put me up.” Restore the authored cockpit families from before `6c89a4a`: their own dashboard shape, cluster artwork, wheel/hub, centre console, vents and trim. Never replace these with one generic shell. Use real-car proportions, roughly **60–75% windscreen/road (about 65–70%)**, a view of the road within **10 metres** ahead, readable instruments, substantial pillars/header/dashboard and a visible rotating wheel with its shared-atlas badge. Native bus/truck cabs retain their own tall furniture; bikes retain their tank/fairing, bars and clocks. Cockpit-only geometry may use **12,000 triangles**. Keep clear inside glazing, dark exterior glazing, no hands/characters, the ±8 cm per-car seat sliders/reset, mounted mirrors, windscreen-plane texture rain and attached wipers. This decision supersedes the thin-band, generic-shell and 80% obstruction rules above; exterior budgets are unchanged.

2026-10-16 final owner/lead clarification: **The percentage is only a proxy for feeling seated in the real car.** Report every configuration’s actual window-opening percentage, with a **soft 55–78% warning band; no exact pixel percentage is a release failure**. Hard failures are road visibility beyond 10 metres, obstructed instruments/telltales, missing distinct authored family interiors, missing own-car structure, or visible hands/characters. Closed cars retain a proper headliner. Bus, truck, compact cabs and bikes retain their real geometry. The aperture metric includes the bonnet viewed through the windscreen; the separate road-ray guard checks that it cannot hide the road ahead.
